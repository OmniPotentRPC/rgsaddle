#+TITLE: Dense symmetric eigensolver
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

~eigh~ copies the symmetric part of its argument, reduces that matrix to bandwidth 64 with tiled Householder panels, chases the bulge to tridiagonal form, and solves the factor with Cuppen's method. The panel reduction and the pipelined chase follow Haidar, Ltaief, and Dongarra (https://doi.org/10.1145/2063384.2063394) and ELPA's two-stage solver (https://doi.org/10.1088/0953-8984/26/21/213201). The chase stores a compact band of half-width 128. A task ~(sweep, step)~ runs when its column window is disjoint from every earlier sweep. It may then take up to 8 further steps while the predicted windows stay disjoint. Waves of fewer than 4 sweeps stay on the calling thread. Reflectors flush in sweep order. The tridiagonal spectrum is Cuppen's divide-and-conquer (https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01396757) as carried out by Gu and Eisenstat (https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895479892241287), with the merge tree fixed from the dimension (leaves at 128, split at n/2). Francis QR (https://doi.org/10.1093/comjnl/4.4.332) is the leaf step. ~partial_eigh~ bisects the same factor and runs inverse iteration for the lowest k pairs. ~k~ equal to the dimension calls ~eigh~.

Every trailing update and the blocked back-transform go through ~tile_gemm~. Each output accumulates over k in ascending order as ~acc = fma(a, b, acc)~, then stores ~alpha * acc~ when beta is zero and ~fma(beta, c, alpha * acc)~ otherwise. The AVX2+FMA, AVX-512, and NEON kernels use the hardware instruction. The scalar path uses ~f64::mul_add~, which is correctly rounded, so it matches those kernels bit for bit. The default x86_64 target does not enable FMA, so a generic ~mul_add~ is a library call even on an FMA host. Householder construction, the WY T factor, the Francis rotation, and the Givens rotation are compiled with the FMA feature when the CPU has it, and that lowers the same chain to one instruction. A CPU without FMA, including the Nehalem job in CI, stays on the library call. On this host the n=256 tridiagonal stage was 0.0269 s while that rotation was still a library call, and 0.0069 s after the specialization. The bits did not change. Each kernel keeps several rows and several columns in registers, and each of those accumulators repeats the same chain. A row panel is packed once and the column tasks share it. The kernel is dispatched at run time (~RGSADDLE_SIMD~ may force ~scalar~, ~avx2~, ~avx512~, or ~neon~). ~avx2~ requires FMA. Tile ownership depends on the dimension and on the tile size, not on the rayon pool. Feature ~par~ runs those tiles on the pool. Without ~par~ the same partition runs on one thread.

The band width is 64, the WY width is 64, and the chase half-width is 128. Those three constants are part of the bit definition. They do not depend on the instruction set, the core count, or the thread count. A WY width of 128 was slower on this host: one rep at n=3000 and one thread took 13.40 s, against 12.01 s at width 64. The wider block grows the union of the row supports, so the back-transform multiplies more entries and the scalar T factor is larger. ~tests/golden/eigh.sha256~ was regenerated from the scalar kernel at one thread after that choice. AVX2+FMA, AVX-512, NEON, and pools of 1, 2, 3, and 8 threads match it. ~eigh_unpinned~ (feature ~faer-unpinned~, off by default) calls faer with the caller's thread count. Its bits depend on the CPU and the thread count. ~eigh~ does not.

Eigenvalues ascend. Each column is signed so that its lowest-index largest-magnitude component is positive. Lanczos ~lowest_ritz~ and ~jacobi_eigen~ call ~partial_eigh~ and ~eigh~.

Wall time depends on the dimension, so one matrix per spectrum class is enough. The tables below are that one draw. They are medians of 7 interleaved runs. The range is the maximum minus the minimum. Efficiency is the one-thread median divided by the product of the thread count and that count's median. The host is an 8-core Intel Xeon (family 6, model 207) with one thread per core, AVX-512, 48 KB of L1 data per core, 2 MB of L2 per core, and 320 MB of L3.

* Why one thread is not within 1.5 times faer

The one-thread targets are 1.5 times faer ~Par::Seq~: 5.072 s at n=3000 and 0.675 s at n=1500. The measured medians are 11.497 s and 1.782 s, which are 3.40 and 3.96 times faer. Eight threads at n=3000 take 4.151 s, above faer's 3.382 s. E(8) there is 0.346. Eight threads at n=1500 take 0.790 s, above faer's 0.450 s.

The back-transform is 6.156 s of the 11.497 s solve. One profiled run of the same size split that step into 1174 WY panels, on average 178 rows by 64 columns. ~Y = V^T Q~ ran at 64.6 GFLOP/s (1.24 s) and ~Z = T Y~ at 64.4 GFLOP/s (0.44 s). ~Q -= V Z~, whose reduction length is the WY width, ran at 16.6 GFLOP/s (4.81 s). Copying those rows into a contiguous buffer, running the same product, and copying them back took 5.52 s and did not raise the throughput. This host issues two AVX-512 fused multiply-adds per cycle. 16.6 GFLOP/s is a small fraction of one core at that peak. The two skinny products in the same step already run at about 65 GFLOP/s, so the short reduction is the part that misses.

The bulge is 1.025 s at one thread and 0.837 s at eight. A sweep's window covers the reflector plus a margin of one band width on either side, so only a few sweeps have disjoint columns. The band reduction is a sequence of panels: 1.741 s at one thread and 1.334 s at eight.

E(8) of 0.6 at a one-thread time of 11.497 s would need an eight-thread time under 2.395 s. At eight threads the band and the bulge are already 2.171 s, before the tridiagonal solve (0.617 s) and the back-transform (1.044 s).

~eigh~ stays the default. ~eigh_unpinned~ remains the opt-in when the caller wants faer's speed and can accept bits that depend on the CPU and the thread count.
