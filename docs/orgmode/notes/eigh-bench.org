#+TITLE: Dense symmetric eigensolver
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

~eigh~ copies the symmetric part of its argument, reduces that matrix to bandwidth 64 with tiled Householder panels, chases the bulge to tridiagonal form, and solves the factor with Cuppen's method. The panel reduction and the pipelined chase follow Haidar, Ltaief, and Dongarra (https://doi.org/10.1145/2063384.2063394). The chase stores a compact band of half-width 128. Several sweeps stay in flight: a sweep runs its next kernel only after every earlier sweep's columns lie strictly to the right. The tridiagonal spectrum is Cuppen's divide-and-conquer (https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01396757) as carried out by Gu and Eisenstat (https://doi.org/10.1137/S0895479892241287), with the merge tree fixed from the dimension (leaves at 128, split at n/2). Francis QR (https://doi.org/10.1093/comjnl/4.4.332) is the leaf step. ~partial_eigh~ bisects the same factor and runs inverse iteration for the lowest k pairs. ~k~ equal to the dimension calls ~eigh~.

Every trailing update and the blocked back-transform go through ~tile_gemm~. Each output accumulates over k in ascending order with a separate multiply and add. The kernel is dispatched at run time (~RGSADDLE_SIMD~ may force ~scalar~, ~avx2~, ~avx512~, or ~neon~). Tile ownership depends on the dimension and on the tile size, not on the rayon pool. Feature ~par~ runs those tiles on the pool. Without ~par~ the same partition runs on one thread. Chase reflectors from consecutive sweeps at one band position form a WY block of width at most 64, applied with three products on fixed column tiles. ~eigh_unpinned~ (feature ~faer-unpinned~, off by default) calls faer with the caller's thread count. Its bits depend on the CPU and the thread count. ~eigh~ does not.

Eigenvalues ascend. Each column is signed so that its lowest-index largest-magnitude component is positive. Lanczos ~lowest_ritz~ and ~jacobi_eigen~ call ~partial_eigh~ and ~eigh~.

Wall time depends on the dimension, so one matrix per spectrum class is enough. The tables below are that one draw. They are medians of 7 interleaved runs. The range is the maximum minus the minimum. Efficiency is the one-thread median divided by the product of the thread count and that count's median. The host is an 8-core Intel Xeon with one thread per core and AVX-512.
