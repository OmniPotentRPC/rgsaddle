#+TITLE: Grow a band with AutoNEB
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

AutoNEB starts from the endpoints, inserts an image where the band is coarsest, relaxes that neighbourhood, and climbs at the end. The climbing image and the dimer are one Householder reflection: Goswami, Gunde, and Jónsson, Front. Chem. (2026), doi:10.3389/fchem.2026.1807063. The band and this climb stage follow that step, then Chill et al., Modelling Simul. Mater. Sci. Eng. 22, 055002 (2014), doi:10.1088/0965-0393/22/5/055002, with eOn v2.9.0.1, doi:10.5281/zenodo.18529495, then Kolsbjerg, Groves, and Hammer, J. Chem. Phys. 145, 094107 (2016), doi:10.1063/1.4961868.

On the double well, AutoNEB used more force calls than one fixed CI-NEB: 1.42 times as many, median 71 against 52, with a 95% BCa interval of 1.33 to 1.52 over 32 paired seeds. Prefer CI-NEB for that single barrier. Müller-Brown from B to C has mean ratio 0.46 (median 268 against 762, interval 0.35 to 0.79) over 31 paired seeds. The two-barrier path from A to B through the intermediate minimum has mean ratio 0.95, interval 0.81 to 1.23, medians 643 and 740, over 32 paired seeds. A pair with a censored side is dropped. Zoom-NEB is the growth method under development.

* Minimal example
~BandConfig::autoneb~ carries the tuning. ~None~ is a fixed band. ~AutoNeb::new~ reads that tuning, or the defaults when it is ~None~.

#+begin_src rust
let mut config = BandConfig::default();
config.autoneb = Some(AutoNebTuning {
    n_simul: 3,
    n_max: 7,
    ..AutoNebTuning::default()
});
let mut search = AutoNeb::new(config, endpoints)?;
let report = search.run(&surface)?;
#+end_src

~run~ is one shot. A second call is refused. The endpoints never move.

* What the knobs do
The knobs are the fields of =BandConfig::autoneb=.

| Field                | Default | ASE name             | Role                                                                                     |
|----------------------+---------+----------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| ~n_simul~            | 3       | ~n_simul~            | Free images in a local window. The window pins one image on each side.                  |
| ~n_max~              | 7       | ~n_max~              | Images at the end, endpoints included. At least ~n_simul + 2~.                           |
| ~local_force_tol~    | 0.1     | ~fmax[0]~            | Force stop for every window before the climb.                                           |
| ~local_max_steps~    | 100     | ~maxsteps[0]~        | Step cap for those windows.                                                              |
| ~climb_max_steps~    | 2000    | ~maxsteps[1]~        | Step cap for the climbing window.                                                        |
| ~space_energy_ratio~ | 0.5     | ~space_energy_ratio~ | Threshold between the longest segment and the peak-weighted energy gap.                 |
| ~climb~              | on      | ~climb~              | Arm the climbing image after the last insertion.                                        |
| ~full_band~          | off     |                      | Also relax the full band after the climbing window. ASE stops without this stage.       |
| ~threads~            | 1       |                      | Image-evaluation threads. ASE's world size is the nearest name, and it is not this knob. |
| ~record_samples~     | off     |                      | Store one ~f64~ per degree of freedom per force call. Off stores nothing and takes no lock. |

Construction fills the band to ~n_simul + 2~ by cutting the longest segment, one image at a time. Each image is the minimum-image midpoint. ASE's AutoNEB, given only the two endpoints, places ~n_simul~ images in one equal-spaced interpolate. The two fills meet on a unit segment when ~n_simul~ is 1 or 3, and they differ for 2.

~run~ then relaxes a local window, inserts one image, and repeats until ~n_max~. The window is ASE's ~which_images_to_run_on~. ~nneb~ starts at ~h - 1 - n_simul / 2~, where ~h~ is the highest-energy image, endpoints included. Ties keep the lower index. The four clamps are ~max(nneb, 0)~, ~min(nneb, n - n_simul - 2)~, ~min(nneb, first_missing - 1)~, and ~max(nneb + n_simul, last_missing) - n_simul~. ~first_missing~ and ~last_missing~ look only at interior images. The ends then walk outward until both hold an energy.

The peak-weighted gap of a segment is the absolute value of ~(E[j+1] - E[j]) * (E[j+1] + E[j] - 2 E_min) / (2 (E_max - E_min))~. ~f_R~ is the longest length over the endpoint separation. ~g_R~ is the largest weight over ~(E_max - E_min)~. The longest segment is cut when ~g_R~ is not positive or ~f_R / g_R~ exceeds ~space_energy_ratio~. Otherwise the largest weight is cut. Ties keep the lower index, including ~+0.0~ against ~-0.0~.

Climbing uses ~BandConfig::climbing~. The default trigger factor is 0.5 and the trigger force is 0. The climbing window and, when ~full_band~ is set, the full band stop at ~BandConfig::force_tol~. Buildup leaves climbing off. The default leaves ~full_band~ off and stops after the climbing window, as ASE and the paper do. The extra stage costs calls: on the same budget comparison its median was 30 on the double well, 315 from B to C, and 285 from A to B, and it raised the two-barrier call ratio to 1.70 over 31 paired seeds. A pair with a censored side is dropped.

* How it differs from ASE
The inserted image is the minimum-image midpoint. The fill from two endpoints cuts one midpoint at a time, while ASE places ~n_simul~ images in one interpolate. The Müller-Brown comparison uses FIRE 1.0, ASE's original FIRE, with this crate's step cap of 2.5 times the starting step. Both codes then insert at index 2 and then at index 1.

* One session per unchanged window
An unchanged window keeps its ~BandSession~, so the L-BFGS pairs survive the moment climbing is armed on those same images. A new index set starts with empty memory. The optional full band is a new session when it is not the same index set as the local window.

* Force calls and threads
~AutoNebReport::surface_rows~ counts force calls. One row of ~BandSurface::eval~ is one call. Known endpoint energies are seeded into a new window, so those two images are not evaluated again. A window that stops on its step cap clears only the free images.

~threads~ changes speed only for a surface whose rows do not depend on the batch they arrive in. There is no SIMD path. Above 1, one thread pool lasts for the whole search. Each evaluation is split into contiguous blocks, one per rank, and the block is stored at its image index after every rank has finished. The inserted image does not depend on which block finished first. A warm step evaluates the free images of the window, so that step is capped by ~n_simul~. A release timing runs AutoNEB to convergence with L-BFGS, memory 10 and ~max_move~ 0.1, on the double well and on Müller-Brown from B to C, with at least 1 ms of spin per row and the median of five interleaved repetitions. FIRE with the band default ~k = 5~ does not bring the ~n_simul~ 8 double well under the force tolerance inside the climb cap, because the fill multiplies ~k~ by ~n_simul + 1~. The timed runs use L-BFGS. Pair FIRE on that well with ~k = 0.1~, which is ASE's spring. The ~n_simul~ 3 double-well timing path is not the straight MEP: the middle image starts at ~(0.15, 0.45, 0)~. The straight path was 11 force calls and went from 12.1 ms to 4.55 ms at 8 threads. At ~n_simul~ 8 it is 378 calls, with 46 batches of 8 free images, and goes from 416 ms to 56.7 ms (7.35 times, efficiency 0.92). Müller-Brown from B to C at ~n_simul~ 3 is 263 calls and goes from 291 ms to 108 ms. At ~n_simul~ 8 it is 1602 calls, with 199 batches of 8, and goes from 1765 ms to 231 ms (7.63 times, efficiency 0.95). A pool microbenchmark of 16 images is separate. Its spawn-and-join cost is 0.015 ms per call at 8 threads.

One thread evaluates the batch in a single call. The surface then sees one block per call when ~threads~ is above 1. A host that tells a whole-band evaluation from an interior evaluation by the row count should leave ~threads~ at 1.

* Springs
The search keeps a private per-segment spring list. A fill from two endpoints sets every segment to ~k * (n_simul + 1)~. Each later insertion replaces the cut segment with two segments of twice that constant. A start that already holds more than two images doubles on every fill insert. Energy-weighted springs are recomputed from the energies of the assembly in progress, so an insertion does not refuse them. A fixed weighted list or an Onsager-Machlup spring supplied on ~BandConfig~ is refused. The refusal is temporary. The solid-state block is not part of this loop.

FIRE with the band default ~k = 5~ on the ~n_simul~ 8, ~n_max~ 12 double well stops at the climb cap with max force 139. The fill has multiplied the constant by ~n_simul + 1~, so the climb sees 45. On that band the largest non-climbing projected component is the spring, and the perpendicular true force on that image is 0. ~k = 0.5~ and ASE's ~k = 0.1~ both converge. The band default stays 5.
