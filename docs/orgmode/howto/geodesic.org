#+TITLE: Geodesic initial paths
#+OPTIONS: toc:nil num:nil

#+begin_export rst
:cite:`zhuGeodesicInterpolation2019`
#+end_export

~geodesic_path~ shortens an initial path in a redundant coordinate chart before any potential evaluation.
Zhu, Thompson, and Martinez, J. Chem. Phys. 150, 164103 (2019), doi:10.1063/1.5090303, define the length of one Cartesian segment by the two point midpoint formula (their Eq. 17): the redundant coordinate distance from each end to the image of the Cartesian midpoint.
On a flat chart that length is the polyline length, and the geodesic is the straight line (Appendix A).
The lower bound is the sum of the coordinate chords between segment ends (Eq. B5).
The upper bound subdivides each Cartesian segment into ~bound_samples~ equal pieces (Eq. B18). The paper uses ten.

The chart implements ~GeodesicChart~. ~eval~ writes ~q(x)~ and the Wilson B matrix ~dq/dx~, one row per redundant coordinate.
~MorsePairChart~ is Eq. (7): \(q = \exp(-\alpha (r - r_e) / r_e) + \beta r_e / r\), with ~alpha~ 1.7, ~beta~ 0.01, and ~r_e~ the sum of covalent radii.
~MORSE_ALPHA~ and ~MORSE_BETA~ hold those two values, and ~with_radii~ replaces the radii of an existing chart.
Pairs are lexicographic. A periodic ~cell~ (a ~minimage::Cell~) takes every pair displacement through ~Cell::displacement~. A pair distance at or below the distance floor is rejected rather than shifted.

~GeodesicConfig~ and ~MorsePairChart~ are non-exhaustive.
Start from ~GeodesicConfig::default~ or ~MorsePairChart::new~ and chain the ~with_*~ setters.
The default stop is a pass displacement of 1e-6, a length gradient norm of 1e-6, and an L-BFGS step of 0.5 in the path's unit.
The default bounds are 0.95 and 1.1, the paper's insertion thresholds.
~with_lower_ratio~ and ~with_upper_ratio~ change them, and ~with_max_sweeps~ caps the passes.

~geodesic_path~ keeps the first and last images fixed.
Each interior image is one L-BFGS minimization of the total length, using the analytic gradient of their Algorithm 1.
Images are swept forward and then back until the largest displacement of a pass is at or below ~step_tol~.
If the bounds are still loose, Cartesian midpoints are inserted on the loose segments, up to ~max_images~, and the sweeps start again.
A trial the chart refuses is discarded. That image keeps its last accepted point.
A chart that returns a ~cell~ from ~GeodesicChart::cell~ marks the rows as one 3-vector per atom in a periodic box.
The Cartesian midpoint of Eq. (17), the subdivision samples of Eq. (B18), and the images inserted between two images all move along ~Cell::displacement~ of their two ends, so a segment that crosses the box takes the short way.
The cell should be tilt-reduced, the engine convention of minimage.

~geodesic_interpolate~ builds the first path from the reactant and the product by their Algorithm 3.
With three or more images the target is the average of the redundant coordinates, \(q_{\mathrm{mid}} = (q(A) + q(B)) / 2\), and not the coordinates of the Cartesian average.
Ten starts alternate between the reactant (odd starts) and the product (even starts), each with Gaussian noise of scale 0.1 in the path's unit from a generator seeded by ~GeodesicConfig::seed~ (~with_seed~).
Each start minimizes \(|q(x) - q_{\mathrm{mid}}|^2\), and the shortest three point path \([A, x, B]\) wins.
The geodesic procedure then adjusts that middle image on the three point path with no insertion, and the result is copied onto the thirds of the images.
The same seed repeats the path.
~geodesic_path~ then minimizes the length.
~geodesic_lengths~ reports the length and both bounds without moving the path, as ~GeodesicLengths~.
~geodesic_path~ and ~geodesic_interpolate~ return the path with a ~GeodesicReport~: the length, both bounds, the image count, and the passes run.

On the Morse pair chart a dissociation whose distance only increases has length ~|q(end) - q(start)|~, the equality case of Eq. (B5).
~tests/geodesic_path.rs~ checks that length for ~r_e = 2~ from ~r = 2~ to ~r = 6~, and the straight segment of length 5 on a flat chart (Appendix A).

From C, ~rgsaddle_geodesic_config_default~ fills ~rgsaddle_geodesic_config_t~, ~rgsaddle_geodesic_path~ takes a host chart and an optional row-major cell, and ~rgsaddle_geodesic_morse~ uses Eq. (7).
A host chart given a cell applies the same minimum image to its own distances.
A NULL config selects the defaults. ~length~, ~lower~, and ~upper~ may be NULL.
