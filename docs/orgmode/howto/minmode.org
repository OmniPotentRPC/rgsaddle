* Runnable example: dimer, Lanczos, soft-mode seeding, and surrogate Hessian actions (GP methods)

The dimer and Lanczos minimum-mode searches share one Householder reflection of the force: Goswami, Gunde, and Jónsson, Front. Chem. 14, 1807063 (2026), doi:10.3389/fchem.2026.1807063, Eq. 9, and Goswami, arXiv:2603.10992 (2026), doi:10.48550/arXiv.2603.10992, Eqs. 2.14-2.16. Gaussian process (GP) accelerated saddle searches and adaptive pruning of the GP training set follow Goswami, Masterov, Kamath, Peña-Torres, and Jónsson, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 21, 7935 (2025), doi:10.1021/acs.jctc.5c00866, and Goswami and Jónsson, ChemPhysChem 27, e202500730 (2026), doi:10.1002/cphc.202500730, with performance and force-call accounting from Goswami, AIP Advances 15, 085210 (2025), doi:10.1063/5.0283639. The dimer rotation follows Henkelman and Jónsson, J. Chem. Phys. 111, 7010 (1999), doi:10.1063/1.480097, Heyden, Bell, and Keil, J. Chem. Phys. 123, 224101 (2005), doi:10.1063/1.2104507, and Kästner and Sherwood, J. Chem. Phys. 128, 014106 (2008), doi:10.1063/1.2815812; the Lanczos mode estimate follows Olsen, Kroes, Henkelman, Arnaldsson, and Jónsson, J. Chem. Phys. 121, 9776 (2004), doi:10.1063/1.1809574. ~soft_mode_then_full_directions~ (~SoftModeDirectionPlan~, ~spectral_gap_rank~) recovers a sparse Hessian at a minimum, picks the soft-subspace rank from the largest spectral gap, and emits unit initial directions.

A Gaussian-process surrogate or analytic potential implements ~PointSurface~ and can supply exact directional Hessian actions through ~PointSurface::hessian_vector~ (or ~WithHessian~ in ~indexk~) so Lanczos and block-Lanczos mode estimates run on the surrogate without finite-difference probes, while ~MinModeSession::reset~ drops optimizer history at each surrogate model-update boundary.

#+begin_src rust
use ndarray::{Array1, ArrayView1, array};
use rgsaddle::{
    MinModeConfig, MinModeKind, MinModeSession, MinModeStatus, PointSurface, SaddleError,
};

struct SurrogateDoubleWell;

impl PointSurface for SurrogateDoubleWell {
    fn eval(&self, x: ArrayView1<f64>) -> Result<(f64, Array1<f64>), SaddleError> {
        let (px, py, pz) = (x[0], x[1], x[2]);
        let e = (px * px - 1.0).powi(2) + 2.0 * py * py + 2.0 * pz * pz;
        Ok((e, array![4.0 * px * (px * px - 1.0), 4.0 * py, 4.0 * pz]))
    }

    fn hessian_vector(
        &self,
        x: ArrayView1<f64>,
        v: ArrayView1<f64>,
    ) -> Result<Option<Array1<f64>>, SaddleError> {
        let hxx = 12.0 * x[0] * x[0] - 4.0;
        Ok(Some(array![hxx * v[0], 4.0 * v[1], 4.0 * v[2]]))
    }
}

fn main() -> Result<(), SaddleError> {
    let start = array![0.3, 0.2, 0.0];
    let seed = array![1.0, 0.0, 0.0];

    let mut dimer = MinModeSession::new(
        MinModeConfig::default().with_kind(MinModeKind::Dimer),
        start.clone(),
        seed.clone(),
    )?;
    let d_rep = dimer.run(&SurrogateDoubleWell, 200)?;
    assert_eq!(d_rep.status, MinModeStatus::Converged);

    let mut lanczos = MinModeSession::new(
        MinModeConfig::default().with_kind(MinModeKind::Lanczos),
        start,
        seed,
    )?;
    let l_rep = lanczos.run(&SurrogateDoubleWell, 200)?;
    assert_eq!(l_rep.status, MinModeStatus::Converged);
    Ok(())
}
#+end_src

~MinModeSession~ estimates a low-curvature direction by dimer rotation or Lanczos, then advances on the min-mode force. L-BFGS takes ~Accept::Step~ and rgmin's NEB guards: the reflected force is not a potential gradient (Goswami, Gunde, and Jónsson, Front. Chem. (2026), doi:10.3389/fchem.2026.1807063, Eq. 9), an empty memory scales the step by 0.01, and a two-loop step that reaches the per-atom cap or faces away from the force drops its pairs. In Rust, a ~MinModeConfig~ with ~rotation_tol < 1e-4~ drops the default five-degree dimer angle stop unless ~with_rotation_angle_tol~ set it explicitly. Lanczos reads ~rotation_tol~ as a dimensionless tolerance on the lowest Ritz residual divided by the absolute Ritz value and, while ~set_lanczos_rel_tol~ is unset, as its relative Ritz-value tolerance. A Ritz value whose magnitude is at most 1e-14 of the curvature scale, the largest Hessian action norm the estimate has formed, divides the residual by that scale. Only in that unset case and with ~rotation_tol >= 1e-4~ does Lanczos infer the classic torque window, which stops once two vectors leave that ratio under 1, dividing by the same scale when the Ritz value is that small. A repeat visit whose Rayleigh quotient still agrees with the retained curvature returns after one product (Olsen, Kroes, Henkelman, Arnaldsson, and Jónsson, J. Chem. Phys. 121, 9776 (2004), doi:10.1063/1.1809574; eOn ~quit_early~). While the curvature along that mode is not negative the step moves the largest atom by ~max_move~ along the mode, as eOn's FIRE and L-BFGS do, and the perpendicular force is dropped. Under ~set_cg_translate~ that step has Euclidean norm ~max_move~, as in eOn's conjugate gradients. A parallel gradient within half that step of the minimum of the parabola along the mode keeps the mode's orientation. Once the curvature is negative the perpendicular force stays and the parallel part is reflected, and the L-BFGS pairs and the FIRE velocity survive a mode turn inside 45 degrees. A turn past that cone drops the memory and zeros the FIRE velocity. FIRE opens at timestep 1.0 and has a ceiling of 2.5. When the per-atom cap shortens a negative-curvature step and the arrival is no longer negative, the move is replaced from the last negative point by 0.01 times the reflected force, the empty-memory scale of the L-BFGS guard. A capped step that stays negative is kept. L-BFGS is unchanged, and no C field was added. Lanczos uses the same translation. ~FiniteDifference~ selects forward or central Hessian actions. A surface can supply analytic actions and excluded directions through ~PointSurface~.

The reported force norm must satisfy the selected ~ForceGate~, and the estimated curvature must be negative. If a translation reaches a stationary point, the session measures curvature at that accepted position before reporting convergence. This directional test does not certify the full Hessian index; the saddle check below does.

~set_kappa~ selects the basin constraint. ~set_highs~ selects the optional feasible-step control. Call ~reset~ at a surface change and use ~set_force_gate~ to select the physical stopping norm.

** Certify the saddle index

~MinModeConfig::saddle_check~ holds a ~SaddleIndexCheck~, on by default.
At the first converged step at a position the session estimates the lowest curvatures there by block Lanczos on forward-difference Hessian actions, one gradient each, seeded with its own mode and pseudo-random vectors, off the directions that ~PointSurface::excluded_modes~ names; ~index~ counts those under ~-negative_tol~ (1e-3).
The controls and the method are those of the band's check in =howto/band=.
A coordinate that is zero in the gradient and stays zero under one forward action along all such coordinates is left out as well, so a host that zeroes the force on the atoms it holds needs no exclusion for them.
~MinModeReport::saddle_index~ carries the result and its evaluations count in ~MinModeReport::evaluations~; ~MinModeSession::saddle_index~ returns it while the session stays at that position, and ~saddle_evaluations~ and ~saddle_escapes~ sum the session's.
The search takes the same steps with the check on or off, and ~with_saddle_check(None)~ or ~set_saddle_check(None)~ turns it off.
A first-order saddle has one negative Hessian eigenvalue, the count eOn requires of the saddle Hessian it builds for a prefactor.
A start that shares a symmetry with the surface can stop the search on a saddle of higher index, since a mode the symmetry keeps out of the force never moves the point; Zhang and Du, SIAM J. Numer. Anal. 50, 1899 (2012), doi:10.1137/110843149, Theorem 3.2, show that such a point is a fixed point of the Euler scheme of shrinking dimer dynamics, though not a contractive one.

With ~SaddleIndexCheck::escape~ set to a ~SaddleEscape~, a converged point of index two or more keeps the negative Ritz vector closest to the session's mode as the reaction mode, moves by ~push~ (0.1, the largest per-atom displacement) along the other negative modes, each to the side of lower energy that two evaluations find, and searches again from there along the reaction mode.
That is the downward search of Yin, Wang, Chen, Zhang, and Zhang, Phys. Rev. Lett. 124, 090601 (2020), doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.124.090601, at index one.
The step that pushes reports ~Running~ with the index it left; a later attempt pushes twice as far, and after ~max_attempts~ (2) the session reports the index it has.
~set_position~ to a new point, ~reset~ and ~set_saddle_check~ start the attempts again.

On the frozen-layer Morse slab of =tests/band_fixed_atoms.rs=, Lanczos started on the stationary point of index 3 that the symmetric band converges to, with the band tangent as its mode and ~force_tol~ 1e-4, certifies index 3, pushes once, and converges on the first-order saddle at -50.6761672669 with index 1, in 1292 evaluations, 126 of them for the two certificates and the push.
At ~force_tol~ 1e-3 the dimer and Lanczos both stop after two pushes at index 2, near -50.67580, with curvatures -1.147 and -0.0076: the force falls under the tolerance on that soft second mode before the search leaves it.
The band's escape, whose climb steps on a quasi-Newton model, reaches the first-order saddle at 1e-3 from the same band (=howto/band=).

** Sweep every Hessian mode of a minimum

~hessian_mode_sweep_directions~ seeds a saddle search from the Hessian modes of a minimum, soft to stiff, where ~soft_mode_then_full_directions~ draws random vectors. It recovers the Hessian by central differences over an all-pairs sparsity pattern and diagonalizes it in full, so it costs ~6 N~ gradient evaluations, ~O((3N)^3)~ time and ~O((3N)^2)~ memory for ~N~ atoms. Above 128 degrees of freedom the diagonalization needs the ~faer~ feature, and without it the call returns ~SaddleError::Solver~.

~rigid_modes~ is the number of lowest eigenpairs left out, and it assumes they are the rigid modes of a stable minimum. Each eigenvector takes the sign that makes its first largest-magnitude component positive, so the directions do not follow the sign the eigensolver chose. A direction is the mode plus ~carrier~ times itself, with the first copy optionally scaled atom by atom by one row of ~localization_weights~ (entries in ~[0, 1]~, rows assigned to modes cyclically), then normalized. Its squared overlap with the mode is at least ~(carrier / (1 + carrier))^2~, for every finite positive carrier: the combination is formed at a scale that cannot overflow or underflow, so a carrier of 1e160 or a carrier of 1e-13 on an all-zero weight row still gives the mode direction. A direction that cannot be formed, because the Hessian is not finite, is an error and never a stand-in coordinate axis. The Hessian is recovered with one colour per atom, which is the colouring of the all-pairs pattern, so the call skips the colouring search.

The call returns a ~ModeSweepDirectionPlan~. ~eigenvalues~ is the whole spectrum, ascending. ~mode_indices~ and ~directions~ list one entry for every mode that was not skipped, and ~minimum_squared_mode_weight~ is the overlap bound above.

~mode_sweep_schedule(total_modes, draws)~ picks which of those modes to try. The values it returns are positions inside the band ~0..total_modes~, not eigenvector columns. A schedule shorter than the band takes the midpoint of each equal-width stratum, so the longest run of modes that nobody visits, ends included, is at most ~ceil(total_modes / draws)~. A schedule at least as long as the band visits every mode once before it cycles. The arithmetic is integer, so the schedule is the same on every platform. Neither function draws random numbers, so a sweep is reproducible without a seed.

** Run the ART nouveau push, then relax protocol

~ArtNConfig~ is an opt-in protocol around the Lanczos session. The ~mode~ passed to ~MinModeSession::new~ is the random basin-exit direction. Before activation, one outer step pushes along that fixed direction and takes a bounded number of steepest-descent steps with the gradient projected perpendicular to it. Lanczos starts only after ~lanczos_start~ pushes. When its lowest Ritz value is below ~eigenvalue_threshold~, activation starts: each outer step warm-starts Lanczos from the previous mode, takes the bounded \( |F_\parallel|/\max(|\lambda|,\lambda_{\rm floor}) \) displacement toward the saddle, and relaxes perpendicular to that mode. ~MinModeConfig::default()~ has ~artn = None~, so dimer and ordinary Lanczos searches do not enter this code.

These are the primary-source protocol statements, quoted rather than inferred from the later efficiency comparisons.

- Malek and Mousseau write that the configuration is "first pushed along a random direction until a negative eigenvalue appears" and "at each step ... relaxed in the hyperplane perpendicular to it"; after the lowest eigenvalue passes their threshold, it is pushed along that eigenvector while the other directions are minimized (Phys. Rev. E 62, 7723 (2000), doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.62.7723, Sec. II, p. 7724). The same paragraph on Lanczos says its iterative scheme "can use the direction of the previous step as a seed".
- Marinica, Willaime, and Mousseau use ARTn for the iron interstitial landscape and introduce efficiency modifications in that application (Phys. Rev. B 83, 094119 (2011), doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.83.094119). The implementation-level account by Cancès, Legoll, Marinica, Minoukadeh, and Willaime describes basin relaxation in the hyperplane orthogonal to the fixed random deformation and activation as alternating an eigenvector move with projected steepest descent. It reports 15 Lanczos iterations for each eigenpair calculation (J. Chem. Phys. 130, 114711 (2009), doi:10.1063/1.3088532, Sec. III, pp. 114711-6–8).
- Jay et al. describe the standard workflow as random increments followed by hyperplane relaxation, with Lanczos delayed for "a few iterations"; activation follows the lowest eigenvector and each push is again followed by perpendicular relaxation (J. Chem. Theory Comput. 16, 6726 (2020), doi:10.1021/acs.jctc.0c00541, "Previous ARTn workflow"). The "ARTn parameters" section reports "6 steps of perpendicular relaxations, after each push", the first eigenpair evaluation "after the third pushing step", an activation count "increased from 5 to 25", and 16 Lanczos iterations (Jay et al. 2020, "ARTn parameters"). Equation 2 gives the 0.5 eV/Angstrom squared denominator floor and says the push cap is "set to 0.2 Angstrom". These reported values set the unit-bearing defaults here. ~eigenvalue_threshold~ defaults to -0.01 eV/Angstrom squared, at the conservative end of the commonly used -0.01 to -0.05 range; it remains a setter because the papers use system- and unit-dependent thresholds (Malek and Mousseau use a magnitude of \(10^{-3}\) in Lennard-Jones units, while Cancès et al. tabulate -2 for their iron tests). ~relaxation_step~ is likewise explicit because a steepest-descent scale is unit dependent.

** Keep one session across a host search

A host that climbs with its own optimizer keeps one session for the whole search. At each new centre it calls ~set_position~ (~rgsaddle_minmode_set_position~) with the gradient it already has, so the session does not evaluate the centre, then ~estimate_mode~ (~rgsaddle_minmode_estimate~). The previous mode seeds the next rotation. Dimer rotations also retain their Polak--Ribiere force and direction across a translation; a repeated estimate at exactly one centre restarts that rotational history. A failed surface evaluation leaves the retained rotation history unchanged, so a recoverable callback error does not silently turn the next estimate into a fresh rotation. ~set_mode~ (~rgsaddle_minmode_set_mode~) replaces the seed and clears both rotational history and the carried curvature. ~reset~ clears them at a surface-epoch boundary. All three need ABI minor 5 and do not extend the minimum-mode configuration layout.

Lanczos through ~rgsaddle_minmode_estimate~ advances one Hessian action and keeps the Krylov basis until ~set_position~, ~set_mode~, or ~reset~. Repeat the call until the host's own test on the mode passes. The basis grows to the free dimension rather than stopping at ~krylov_dim~. After k actions the returned mode lies in the k + 1 Lanczos vectors: the last is the residual direction the k-th action already produced. In the Lanczos basis an eigenvector's components are the Lanczos polynomials at its eigenvalue (Parlett, The Symmetric Eigenvalue Problem, SIAM (1998), doi:10.1137/1.9781611971163, Ch. 7), and only the known k rows of the tridiagonal enter. The session evaluates them at an estimate of the lowest eigenvalue: the next Ritz value predicted with the last Lanczos diagonal, extrapolated with the two previous Ritz values by Aitken's delta-squared (Aitken, Proc. R. Soc. Edinburgh 46, 289 (1927), doi:10.1017/S0370164600022070) when those fall with shrinking steps. At the Ritz value the same formula is the plain Ritz vector. No surface call is added, and the reported curvature is still the Ritz value of the k vectors, an upper bound on the lowest eigenvalue. Sella 0.1.1 (Hermes, Sargsyan, Najm, and Zádor, J. Chem. Theory Comput. 15, 6536 (2019), doi:10.1021/acs.jctc.9b00869) expands by the same residual at this stage, where it has no Hessian yet to precondition with, and tests the Ritz vector of the vectors it has applied the Hessian to. On ~lowest-eigenvector/lj38~ that iteration and the plain Ritz vector both take about 16.8 actions per start. ~MinModeSession::estimate_mode~ and ~step~ still finish their own stop, the residual test or the torque window, before a translation (Olsen, Kroes, Henkelman, Arnaldsson, and Jónsson, J. Chem. Phys. 121, 9776 (2004), doi:10.1063/1.1809574). A dimer estimate is the full rotation.

The Python point stencil does not take ~rotation_angle_tol~; its dimer builder explicitly keeps the default five-degree stop at every ~rotation_tol~. ~rotation_tol~ on that stencil defaults to 1e-4.

~set_torque_window~ sets the torque window apart from ~rotation_tol~.
From C ABI minor 32 ~rgsaddle_minmode_set_torque_window~ does the same.
~Some(false)~ keeps a loose residual without the window.
eOn's improved dimer does that with its ~torque_min~ of 0.1.
~Some(true)~ adds the window to a tighter residual or to an improved dimer.
~None~, the default, infers the window from ~rotation_tol~ for Lanczos and for a dimer whose angle stop is off.
Lanczos reads the same window, from C ABI minor 41 as well.
Its first two vectors span the plane of a steepest-descent dimer rotation, and their Ritz vector is that dimer turned to the lowest curvature in the plane (Zeng, Xiao, and Henkelman, J. Chem. Under a host relative tolerance (~set_lanczos_rel_tol~, which the climbing image of a band and the connection driver set), ~None~ leaves the window off and ~Some(true)~ adds it.

** Stop the rotation on the force error it leaves
~set_rotation_stop_length~ turns on the curvature-aware stop of Kästner and Sherwood (J. Chem. Phys. 128, 014106 (2008), doi:10.1063/1.2815812).
From the rotational force \(F\) and the curvature \(C\) the dimer estimates the rotation still to come as \(\omega = \tfrac12 \arctan(F / \lvert C\rvert)\), the trial angle it already uses.
A translation of length \(L\) along a mode that is \(\omega\) off leaves a force error of about \(\lvert C\rvert L \lvert\sin\omega\rvert\), and the rotation stops once that is at most ~force_tol~.
The curvature magnitude taken is the larger of \(\lvert C\rvert\) and the two-mode estimate after the rotation, \(C - F\tan\omega\): halfway between a negative and a positive mode the dimer reads \(C \approx 0\) with 45 degrees still to turn (~validation/rotation_force_stop.py~).
The stop adds to ~rotation_tol~, the angle test, the torque window and ~max_rotations~; whichever fires first ends the rotation.

Lanczos stops on the same bound.
After \(j\) actions the residual of the lowest Ritz pair, \(\lVert H y - \theta y\rVert\), is the rotational force of the Ritz vector \(y\) and \(\theta\) is its curvature, so a positive length also accepts a Ritz pair whose residual satisfies the bound, beside the dimensionless ~rotation_tol~ residual and the relative Ritz-value test.

The length is the host's translation scale; 0, the default, turns the stop off, and a negative or non-finite length is an error.
From C ABI minor 38 ~rgsaddle_minmode_set_rotation_stop_length~ does the same, the C++ ~MinMode~ wrapper has ~set_rotation_stop_length~, and Python's ~MinMode~ takes ~rotation_stop_length~.

** Seed the first translation from the mode curvature

~MinModeConfig::seed_curvature~ is off unless a host sets it, and ~MinModeSession::set_seed_curvature~ does the same on a session that already exists. The C configuration and the point stencil leave it off, so those searches step as before. While the curvature ~λ~ along the mode is negative, no basin constraint is set, and the displacement ~F/|λ|~ fits in ~max_move~, one translation uses that scale. L-BFGS does it when its memory is still empty, and stores the secant of that step. FIRE does it on its first move. Every later step is the stepper's own update, still capped by ~max_move~. A step that would only hit the cap is left to the stepper: that capped move does not depend on ~|λ|~. ~reset~ arms the seed again.

eOn 3.6.0 is the source of the L-BFGS half. ~MinModeObjectiveFunction::knownCurvature~ returns ~-λ~ once the mode curvature is negative, and ~LBFGS::getStep~ sets ~H0 = 1/known~ on the optimizer's first iteration (https://github.com/TheochemUI/eOn/blob/v3.6.0/client/MinModeSaddleSearch.cpp and ~client/LBFGS.cpp~). The record doi:10.5281/zenodo.18529496 is TheochemUI/eOn v2.9.0.1. In that release, and at tag v2.17.0, the opening scale is still a finite difference along the force, so that record is not this seed. After the first step the scale is ~s·y / y·y~ (Liu and Nocedal, Math. Program. 45, 503 (1989), doi:10.1007/BF01589116). The reflected force those steps follow is Equation 3 of Goswami, Gunde, and Jónsson, Front. Chem. 14, 1807063 (2026), doi:10.3389/fchem.2026.1807063. FIRE's later steps are its own integrator (Bitzek, Koskinen, Gähler, Moseler, and Gumbsch, Phys. Rev. Lett. 97, 170201 (2006), doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.97.170201).

** A stalled FIRE step on a stiff mode

FIRE 2.0 keeps its opening timestep through the initial delay (Guénolé, Nöhring, Vaid, Houllé, Xie, Prakash, and Bitzek, Comput. Mater. Sci. 175, 109584 (2020), doi:10.1016/j.commatsci.2020.109584). Minimum mode FIRE opens at 1.0 with a ceiling of 2.5, matching eOn. On a stiff mode the harmonic length ~|F|/|λ|~ can still be far under the atom cap. ~set_fire_curvature~, on by default, waits until neither the force nor the energy has dropped for 2000 steps and ~|λ|~ is past 4, then takes one step of length ~|F|/slope~ along the reflected force of Goswami, Gunde, and Jónsson, Front. Chem. 14, 1807063 (2026), doi:10.3389/fchem.2026.1807063, at most 0.05, provided the energy does not rise by more than 1e-3. A shorter trial is tried when that step would. The probe then stays in place while the curvature remains that steep, so the integrator cannot rebuild the cycle. A search that is still descending is unchanged. ~set_fire_curvature(false)~ is the previous integrator. L-BFGS does not read the switch. No C field was added. ~RGSADDLE_ABI_MINOR~ is 34.

~set_cg_translate~ replaces the translator with a Polak-Ribiere step perpendicular to the mode and a Newton step along it (Kästner and Sherwood, J. Chem. Phys. 128, 014106 (2008), doi:10.1063/1.2815812; Baker, J. Comput. Chem. 7, 385 (1986), doi:10.1002/jcc.540070402). ~set_lbfgs_project~ drops the component of each stored L-BFGS pair along the mode after a turn inside the 45 degree cone. Both are off.

** Buffers
A dimer estimate keeps one set of buffers for all its rotations: the
rotational force, its conjugate direction, and the trial and rotated
dimers.
Lanczos keeps its Krylov vectors, residual, coefficients and the tables
of its Ritz solve in the session, and moves each Hessian action into the
Krylov block it hands back.
A finite-difference action shifts the point in a buffer of its call and
forms the difference in the gradient the surface returned.
Outside the surface, a longer rotation or a larger Krylov space
allocates nothing more, and every estimate and step is bitwise what it
was.
The dimer is the reflection of Goswami, Gunde and Jonsson,
Front. Chem. 14 (2026), doi:10.3389/fchem.2026.1807063, Eq. 9,
and Goswami (2026), arXiv:2603.10992, Eqs. 2.14-2.16, on the rotation
of Henkelman and Jonsson, J. Chem. Phys. 111, 7010 (1999),
doi:10.1063/1.480097.
The Lanczos estimate is that of Olsen, Kroes, Henkelman, Arnaldsson
and Jonsson, J. Chem. Phys. 121, 9776 (2004), doi:10.1063/1.1809574.

~examples/band_scaling.rs~ runs up to 300 steps of each search from
the midpoint of its adatom hop, seeded with the hop direction, in the
paired runs of =howto/band=.
The time saved per surface call is fixed, so it shows only where the
surface is cheap: Lanczos runs 1.11 (1.07-1.13) times as fast as the
parent at 38 atoms, 1.04 (1.03-1.04) at 128 and 1.01 (1.00-1.02) at 256,
and the dimer 1.03, 1.02 and 1.02, with 8 to 11 of 12 pairs won.
At 256 atoms both searches converge at step 108, as they do on the
parent.
These runs predate the torque window of minor 41, which changes the
Lanczos searches.
