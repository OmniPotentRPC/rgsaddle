=Stencil::point= builds a minimiser, a dimer, Lanczos, ART, or eigen following. Its defaults are forward difference, =dr = 1e-3=, rotation tolerance =1e-4=, rotation angle five degrees, 20 rotations, Krylov dimension 12, FIRE with opening timestep 1.0, and maximum move 0.2. A dimer whose builder leaves =rotation_angle_tol= unset explicitly keeps the five-degree stop at every =rotation_tol=; setting the angle to zero disables it. Lanczos uses =rotation_tol= as its Ritz residual and relative Ritz-value tolerance and, at =1e-4= or looser, infers the classic torque window while no host relative tolerance is set. Explicit angle values on Lanczos and ART do not add an angle criterion, but they must still be finite and non-negative. When the per-atom cap shortens a negative-curvature FIRE step and the arrival is no longer negative, that step is replaced from the last negative point by 0.01 times the reflected force. Dimer and Lanczos are a =MinModeSession=. Dimer rotational conjugate-gradient history crosses translations. A negative curvature reflects the parallel force. A curvature that is not negative moves the largest atom by the maximum move along the mode and drops the perpendicular force; within half that step of the minimum along the mode it keeps the mode's orientation, for eigen following as well. Henkelman and Jónsson (1999), doi:10.1063/1.480097. Goswami, Gunde, and Jónsson, Front. Chem. (2026), doi:10.3389/fchem.2026.1807063, Eq. 9.

#+begin_src rust
let mut search = Stencil::point(x0)
    .preset(Preset::Dimer)
    .mode(seed)
    .build()?;
let report = search.step(&surface)?;
#+end_src

ART pushes along the seed with perpendicular relaxation, starts Lanczos after =art_push_steps= pushes (default 3; =art_push= in Python and =RGSADDLE_STENCIL_KEY_ART_PUSH= in C), and climbs with Lanczos once the lowest curvature falls under =art_threshold= (default -0.01), matching =ArtNConfig=. Malek and Mousseau (2000), doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.62.7723. Eigen following builds a central-difference Hessian, calls =exact_eigh=, and makes the largest-magnitude component of the lowest mode positive. =build= rejects =Climb::Reflect= on a minimiser, a window on a point, a zero thread count, and ART without a mode solver.

=Stencil::band= checks the array and then returns =band presets stay on feat/band-methods-zoom=. Zoom, energy-weighted springs, and the band side of the builder are that branch. The chain, the mesh (remesh, insert, window), and the point controller are on this branch.

From C, =rgsaddle_stencil_config_new= takes the preset discriminant and fills the =Stencil::point= defaults (from ABI minor 66, =RGSADDLE_STENCIL_KEY_ART_THRESHOLD= is -0.01 and =RGSADDLE_STENCIL_KEY_ART_PUSH= is 3). Unknown keys return =RGSADDLE_INVALID_PARAMETER=. =rgsaddle_last_error= is thread-local. =rgsaddle_stencil_run= loops over =rgsaddle_stencil_step=. The report opens with =struct_size=. The older band and min-mode entries stay step-only, and these stencil symbols entered at ABI minor 16.

From Python, =rgsaddle.Stencil.point(x, preset="dimer")= takes a C-contiguous float64 vector. =step(surface)= releases the thread, and the surface callback re-attaches. =rgsaddle.ase.Point(atoms, preset="dimer").run(fmax, steps)= is an ASE =Optimizer= whose =step= is one stencil step. A second thread that enters the same handle raises =RuntimeError=.

=Chain::evaluate= writes each image from a block of one. Threads 1, 2, 3, and 8 produce the same bytes. A point preset is one row, so its thread count does not change the bytes either.
