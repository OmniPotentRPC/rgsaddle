=Stencil::point= builds a minimiser, a dimer, Lanczos, ART, or eigen following. Its defaults are forward difference, =dr = 1e-3=, rotation tolerance =1e-4=, rotation angle five degrees, 20 rotations, Krylov dimension 12, FIRE with opening timestep 1.0, and maximum move 0.2. A dimer whose builder leaves =rotation_angle_tol= unset explicitly keeps the five-degree stop at every =rotation_tol=; setting the angle to zero disables it. Lanczos reads =rotation_tol= as a dimensionless Ritz residual, divided by the absolute Ritz value, and as its relative Ritz-value tolerance and, at =1e-4= or looser, infers the classic torque window while no host relative tolerance is set. The dimer reads it as a rotational force in curvature units. Explicit angle values on Lanczos and ART do not add an angle criterion, but they must still be finite and non-negative. When the per-atom cap shortens a negative-curvature FIRE step and the arrival is no longer negative, that step is replaced from the last negative point by 0.01 times the reflected force. Dimer and Lanczos are a =MinModeSession=. Dimer rotational conjugate-gradient history crosses translations. A negative curvature reflects the parallel force. A curvature that is not negative moves the largest atom by the maximum move along the mode and drops the perpendicular force; within half that step of the minimum along the mode it keeps the mode's orientation, for eigen following as well. Henkelman and Jónsson (1999), doi:10.1063/1.480097. Goswami, Gunde, and Jónsson, Front. Chem. (2026), doi:10.3389/fchem.2026.1807063, Eq. 9.

#+begin_src rust
let mut search = Stencil::point(x0)
    .preset(Preset::Dimer)
    .mode(seed)
    .build()?;
let report = search.step(&surface)?;
#+end_src

ART pushes along the seed until the curvature falls under =art_threshold= (default 0), then climbs with Lanczos. Malek and Mousseau (2000), doi:10.1103/PhysRevE.62.7723. Eigen following builds a central-difference Hessian, calls =exact_eigh=, and makes the largest-magnitude component of the lowest mode positive. =build= rejects =Climb::Reflect= on a minimiser, a window on a point, a zero thread count, and ART without a mode solver.

=Stencil::band= checks the array and returns =BAND_PRESET_SEAM= (=band presets stay on feat/band-methods-zoom=, kept for compatibility) because band presets are not implemented in the stencil builder. Use =BandSession=, =AutoNeb=, or =OcinebSession= directly for a band search, and =BandConfig::zoom= or =SpringKind::EnergyWeighted= for zoom-NEB and energy-weighted springs. The chain, the mesh (remesh, insert, window), and the point controller are implemented.

From C, =rgsaddle_stencil_config_new= takes the preset discriminant. Unknown keys return =RGSADDLE_INVALID_PARAMETER=. =rgsaddle_last_error= is thread-local. =rgsaddle_stencil_run= loops over =rgsaddle_stencil_step=. The report opens with =struct_size=. The older band and min-mode entries stay step-only, and the ABI minor stays 16.

From Python, =rgsaddle.Stencil.point(x, preset="dimer")= takes a C-contiguous float64 vector. =step(surface)= releases the thread, and the surface callback re-attaches. =rgsaddle.ase.Point(atoms, preset="dimer").run(fmax, steps)= is an ASE =Optimizer= whose =step= is one stencil step. A second thread that enters the same handle raises =RuntimeError=.

=Chain::evaluate= writes each image from a block of one. Threads 1, 2, 3, and 8 produce the same bytes. A point preset is one row, so its thread count does not change the bytes either.
