#+title: Defect-pair hop rates
#+options: toc:nil num:nil

* What is computed
=defect_pair_rates= takes any =PointSurface=, a periodic cell, one relaxed defect geometry, and a catalogue of hops. A hop is a product geometry with a name. The library does not know what a vacancy or a dumbbell is. The caller names the fixed point, the atoms whose centroid is the mobile defect, and a shell label.

For each hop the search

1. minimizes the reactant and the product,
2. climbs from the reactant side of the hop with =SolidDimer=, on a frozen cell or on the joint chart,
3. keeps the saddle only when =IndexCheck= reports exactly one negative projected eigenvalue,
4. pushes off both sides of the unstable mode by a largest-atomic displacement of =push=, minimizes, and accepts the hop when the two clouds of atoms match the reactant and the product (Hausdorff distance, so a permutation of identical atoms still matches),
5. builds an image-dependent-pair-potential path through that saddle, relaxes it with climbing off, then climbs. The climb uses the caller's band method. If that climb runs away, it is repeated with FIRE, or with ODE12r when the caller was already on FIRE,
6. integrates the projected average force along that path and shoots the Bennett–Chandler factor from the free-energy maximum.

AutoNEB, zoom, and image freezing are not used. The band has a fixed number of images.

A hop is =toward= when the mobile centroid moves closer to the fixed defect by more than the tolerance, =away= when it moves farther, and =tangential= otherwise. =classify_separation= is that test.

* The rate
The finite-temperature rate is

: k(T) = κ ν exp(-ΔF / kT)

=ΔF= is the potential of mean force from the reactant hyperplane to the dividing surface, the maximum of the PAFI profile. =ν= is the reactant attempt frequency along the path tangent only,

: ν = (1 / 2π) sqrt( (τ · H τ) / m )

with =τ= the unit tangent at the reactant and =H= the Cartesian Hessian. =κ= is the Bennett–Chandler transmission coefficient. In the harmonic limit =ΔF= holds the transverse vibrational entropy, and this expression agrees with Vineyard's rate when =κ = 1=. Vineyard, J. Phys. Chem. Solids 3, 121 (1957), doi:10.1016/0022-3697(57)90059-8.

The comparison rate is the full Vineyard prefactor,

: k_h(T) = ν_h exp(-ΔE0 / kT),    ν_h = (∏ ν_min) / (∏ ν_sad),

translations removed when the Hessian is the whole cell, the unstable eigenvalue left out of the saddle product. =ΔE0= is the potential difference, or the enthalpy difference when an external stress is set. The harmonic free-energy barrier implied by the same split is

: ΔF_h = ΔE0 - kT ln(ν_h / ν).

=ΔF - ΔF_h= is the anharmonic excess. At equal mass, energy in eV and length in Å, =angular_scale_rad_s= turns =sqrt(λ / m)= into rad/s and the rates are in Hz. A caller in another unit system sets =boltzmann=, =mass= and =angular_scale=.

* PAFI
Swinburne and Marinica, Phys. Rev. Lett. 120, 135503 (2018), doi:10.1103/PhysRevLett.120.135503, give the free-energy gradient along a path without a collective variable. The path =X(s)= is =ArcPath=, the not-a-knot cubic spline of every coordinate through the images: the paper's "spline interpolation for each atomic coordinate" of NEB images (p. 135503-2) and the default end condition of scipy's =CubicSpline=, which their reference code uses. Two images give the chord and three the parabola. The hyperplane at =s= is =(x - X) · t = 0=, with =t = X'= less its center-of-mass part when the center of mass is held. With =ψ = (x - X) · X'' / |t|²=,

: dF/ds = ⟨ (∇E · t) (1 - ψ) ⟩.

The paper's =ψ=, its Eq. (5), is =1 - ψ= here. =PafiMeanForce::ReferenceCode=, the default, averages the first term of its Eq. (8), as LAMMPS =fix pafi= and Swinburne's public driver do. This force-only estimator integrates to the free energy of the flat hyperplanes, =-kT ln ∫ δ((x - X) · t / |t|) exp(-E / kT) dx=. =PafiMeanForce::PublishedEquation8= adds the paper's second term, =kT d/ds ln(|1 - ψ| / |X'|)=, evaluated analytically from =X'=, =X''=, and the one-sided =X'''= of the cubic spline; it refuses a sample when the Eq. (6) locality condition =1 - ψ > 0= fails. =HyperplaneAverage::psi= is the mean of =ψ=. Sampling is overdamped Langevin (Brownian) dynamics in that plane, Eq. (9), stepped as in Leimkuhler and Matthews, Appl. Math. Res. Express 2013, 34 (2013), doi:10.1093/amrx/abs010, Eq. (3), the high-friction limit of their BAOAB step: the noise of a step is the sum of a new Gaussian draw and the draw of the step before. =PafiIntegrator::EulerMaruyama= is the paper's direct Eq. (9) discretization and the reference-code protocol. A step is still one force call. The parallel piece of the drift and of the noise is removed, the plane is restored after every step, and the center of mass can be held. A plane can hold more than one basin, and the walk can cross into another one. With =max_jump= set, 0.4 Å by default, and =window= left at 0, the production run is cut into =blocks= stretches; the thermalized configuration and the end of every stretch are quenched in the plane, along the steepest-descent flow unless =quench= says otherwise, and compared with the plane minimum quenched from the path. One atom farther than =max_jump= from it means the walk has left the basin of the path, and that stretch is sampled again from the end of the last kept one; =HyperplaneAverage::rejected= counts them, and more than =blocks= is an error, since the plane then does not hold the walk in the basin. Swinburne and Marinica's reference code drops a trajectory whose minimum after sampling has moved more than its =MaxJumpThresh= of 0.4 Å the same way. Dropping stretches conditions the walk on staying in the basin for a whole stretch, which takes a little weight from near the ridge. The integral is the trapezoid rule. The standard error comes from block means whose block is at least twice the integrated autocorrelation time of the recorded series, propagated through the trapezoid weights. When =target_error= is set, planes are resampled and inverse-variance combined until the barrier error is at or below that target or =max_rounds= is spent. The dividing surface is the plane of maximum =ΔF=. =zero_temperature_barrier= integrates =∇E · X'= along the spline, which is the energy along it and passes through the image energies; on a converged path that is the zero-temperature limit of the same integral. A finite-temperature rate is stored only when that limit reproduces =ΔE0= within =zero_t_tol= and the band force is under tolerance. The attempt frequency and the Bennett–Chandler normal take the same spline tangent.

Hyperplanes normal to the chords between images do not form one family: the normal jumps at every image, so the planes on the two sides of an image differ and the curvature term is lost. Their average is the mean force on planes that no single free energy is the integral over.

=pafi_frames= puts one plane on every frame of a band and takes every choice the barrier could otherwise depend on from the band. The normal is the unit tangent of the parabola through the frame and its two neighbours, and =dn/ds= the same derivative of the normals; both are of second order in the spacing and the same planes for the band reversed. A chord instead tilts the plane by half the angle the path turns over it, and the thermal part of the barrier then moves with the spacing at first order. The normal is that full tangent with rigid translations removed by =HyperplaneConstraint::orthogonal_to=, and the estimator uses the same vector. A normal that keeps only the atoms carrying some share of the summed squared steps is a different plane: its barrier depends on the share, and =harmonic_mask_shift= is the harmonic map back onto the full tangent. Each frame is relaxed in its plane to =x*_k= at energy =E*_k=, its chain starts there, and with =g = (∇E · ν) (τ · ν - (x - X) · dν/ds)=

: F_k = E*_k - E*_0 + Σ trapezoid(⟨g⟩ - g(x*)).

At zero temperature the sum vanishes and the barrier is the energy of the in-plane minima whatever the spacing, so the trapezoid acts on the thermal part alone. A plane near a saddle can also cut the basin of another state, and a chain left long enough on it falls there. With =window= at least 1 and =max_jump= set, the chain is relaxed in its plane to =jump_tol= every =window= samples; when an atom ends further than =max_jump= from the minimum of the plane, the window is dropped and the chain goes back to where the last kept window ended. A plane that keeps no window in its first pass leaves the basin faster than one window. It halves its window and starts again from its minimum, thermalizing in windows too, and goes on halving, up to four times, while it drops more windows than it keeps: a long window that ends in the basin may still have spent part of its time outside. =average_hyperplane= takes that windowed test when =window= is at least 1, and the quench test above when =window= is 0.

On a straight path =ψ= is zero and the estimator is the mean force. A one-dimensional barrier =(1 - x²)²= integrates to 1. A two-dimensional well =(1 - x²)² + ½ k(x) y²= with =k = 1.5 + 0.5 x= has the exact classical shift =(kT / 2) ln(k(0) / k(-1))=. On its plane =x = 0= the walk is the harmonic mode =k = 1.5= in =y=, and =dF/ds = ⟨y²⟩ / 4 = kT / (4 k)=; at =μ k dt = 1.2=, where the Euler–Maruyama step samples =⟨y²⟩= 2.5 times too wide, the sampled gradient is within four standard errors of it. A bent valley =(1 - x²)² + ½ k (y - a (1 - x²))²= with =a = 0.5= and =k = 4=, at =kT = 0.1= on five images, has curved hyperplanes: on each of nine planes the sampled gradient matches the derivative of =-kT ln Z= of the spline hyperplanes by quadrature, which chord hyperplanes miss by up to 0.18. On the plane =x = 0= of =h (y² - 1)² - b y + c x y= with =h = 0.4=, =b = 0.1= and =c = 0.5=, at =kT = 0.1=, the path sits in the upper of two wells, =2 kT= above the lower behind a ridge =3 kT= high, and =∂E/∂x = c y= tells them apart: with the basin test the gradient is =c ⟨y⟩= over the basin of the path by quadrature, and without it the walk falls into the lower well. Tilted to =b = 0.55=, the upper well does not hold the walk for a stretch and the plane is refused. The ring =A (1 + a sin²(2θ)) (r - 1)² + W sin²(2θ)= has the same curved hop between every pair of neighbouring axes, with the barrier =W + (kT / 2) ln(1 + a)=; on twelve unevenly spaced frames =pafi_frames= gives the same barrier built forward, turned onto the next hop and mirrored, and the reversed band gives the reverse barrier back. On a plane that cuts two basins in =y=, the windowed jump test keeps the average of the basin the chain starts in, also with a first window far longer than the chain stays there. =tests/pafi_analytic.rs= checks these, and that the spline reproduces a cubic and its reversal.

* Bennett–Chandler
Bennett, in Algorithms for Chemical Computations, ACS Symposium Series 46, 63 (1977), doi:10.1021/bk-1977-0046.ch004, and Chandler, J. Chem. Phys. 68, 2959 (1978), doi:10.1063/1.436049, write the recrossing factor as the plateau of the flux correlation. Configurations are the PAFI samples on the dividing surface. Velocities are Maxwell–Boltzmann. The trajectories are inertial Langevin (BAOAB); zero friction is Newtonian.

: κ(t) = ⟨ v_τ(0) θ(s(t)) ⟩ / ⟨ v_τ(0) θ(v_τ(0)) ⟩

=s(t) = (x(t) - x_surface) · τ= and =θ(s)= is 1 on the product side of the surface. Folding the two velocity signs onto the positive flux distribution gives the forward/backward form used by =pafi_transmission=, =κ(t) = ⟨h_P(t) - h_P(-t)⟩=. =τ= is the path tangent from the reactant toward the product, the direction of increasing arc length on a reactant-first path. The opposite normal is not tried, and a negative =κ= is not clamped into =[0, 1]=. The reported =κ= is the last requested checkpoint, with a block standard error. A rate is formed only when =κ= lies in =[0, 1]=.

On the quartic =V = (x² - 1)²= the barrier curvature gives =ω_b = 2= at unit mass. Newtonian trajectories that start at the top with =v > 0= are still on the product side a short time later, so =κ = 1=. With friction =γ = 2=, Kramers, Physica 7, 284 (1940), doi:10.1016/S0031-8914(40)90098-2, gives

: κ = sqrt(1 + α²) - α,    α = γ / (2 ω_b) = 1/2,

which is =sqrt(5)/2 - 1/2 ≈ 0.618=. =tests/bennett_kramers.rs= compares the plateau of =κ(t)= with that value.

=pafi_transmission= is the system-agnostic bridge from PAFI to the
forward/backward shooter. It selects =profile.dividing=, takes the
constrained-equilibrium configurations retained by that plane's PAFI
chain, and delegates to =transmission_coefficient=. The forward half
represents the future and the velocity-reversed backward half the past.
=Counting::Final= is Bennett--Chandler counting; =Counting::FirstCrossing=
is the equivalent positive-flux estimator after the halves commit to
their states. The result reports =κ=, its block standard error, assigned
and unassigned shots, reactive shots, recrossings and force evaluations.
Set the same masses, thermostat friction and temperature as the dynamics
the coefficient corrects. A plane with no retained samples is an error:
increase =PafiConfig::retain= rather than shooting the unsampled path
frame.

* The catalogue
=RateCatalogue::to_json= writes =rgsaddle.defect_pair_rates= version 1. =from_json= reads it back. Each entry is one exit channel. The key is =(shell, direction, hop, temperature, cell_repeats)=. =rate_Hz= is =k(T)= in the caller's frequency unit (Hz when the angular scale is =angular_scale_rad_s=).

amsel is the absorbing-Markov selector for superbasin exit distributions. It does not parse this file itself. A loader groups entries that share a shell, a cell and a temperature, and passes =rate_Hz= to the FPTA or MRM kernel as the first-order exit rate of that channel. =hop= and =direction= are the channel name. =dE0=, =dF=, =dF_error=, =dF_harmonic=, =nu=, =vineyard=, =kappa=, the saddle Cauchy stress and the elastic dipole =P = -V (σ - σ_bulk)= are stored beside the rate so a later run can see what entered =k(T)=.

* Relaxed states
A placement is a starting geometry, not a state. Whether it is a bound pair or has recombined is a property of the minimum it relaxes to, so that minimum has to be reached. =relax_to_tolerance= minimizes in a fixed cell until the largest per-atom force norm, =max_i |F_i|=, is below =RelaxConfig::force_tol= (=DEFAULT_FORCE_TOL=, 1e-3 in the surface's force unit, eV/Å for eV and Å). =RelaxConfig::max_steps= (=DEFAULT_MAX_STEPS=, 10000) is a guard. Reaching it returns =SaddleError::Solver= with the step count and the force reached, never a geometry. The minimizer is any =rgmin::Method=, FIRE 2.0 by default.

Convergence fixes the end state as a minimum, not which minimum. From a start near a basin boundary FIRE, which carries momentum, and L-BFGS, which extrapolates, can each settle below the tolerance in different basins. When the basin is the answer, as it is for recombination, follow the steepest-descent flow with =Method::Ode12r= and check it with a second integration of the same flow, =Method::Steepest= under a smaller per-atom cap.

#+begin_src rust
let relax = rgsaddle::RelaxConfig::default()
    .with_method(rgmin::Method::Ode12r)
    .with_force_tol(1.0e-3)
    .with_max_atom_move(0.08)
    .with_initial_step(0.08);
let done = rgsaddle::relax_to_tolerance(&surface, start, cell, &relax)?;
let occupancy = rgsaddle::site_occupancy(sites.view(), done.positions.view(), cell)?;
let recombined = occupancy.is_perfect();
#+end_src

=site_occupancy= is the Wigner–Seitz test (Nordlund et al., Phys. Rev. B 57, 7556 (1998), doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.57.7556): each atom belongs to the nearest reference site under the minimum image, an empty site is a vacancy and a site with =n= atoms carries =n - 1= interstitials. A Frenkel pair relaxed to a configuration with every site singly occupied has recombined, whatever its local distortion. Neither function knows what the defect is; the caller supplies the surface and the sites.

* Pair networks
=PairNetwork= is the kinetic network of a mobile defect next to a fixed one. A configuration is an integer relative vector and an orientation director, in a unit the caller picks (=a/2= for an fcc lattice), and a point group of integer matrices acts on both. A state is one orbit of configurations, so every symmetry-distinct relative vector and orientation is its own state; nothing is lumped by radius or by axis. Moves carry a kind, a displacement, the orientation before and after, and a saddle director =via= that tells apart two paths between the same configurations. A hop class is one orbit of the unordered pair of configurations with its =via=, so one saddle serves a hop and its reverse and detailed balance holds when rates come from saddle and state energies. =PairNetwork::sites= asks a closure for the rate of each edge, with the hop class, its direction and the source state, and returns =VolumeSite= rows and the hops that leave the radius.

#+begin_src rust
let group = rgsaddle::cubic_group();
let moves = rgsaddle::PairMove::orbit(
    &[
        rgsaddle::PairMove::new("translate", [1, 1, 0], [1, 0, 0], [0, 1, 0], [0, 0, 0]),
        rgsaddle::PairMove::new("rotate", [0, 0, 0], [1, 0, 0], [0, 1, 0], [1, 1, 0]),
    ],
    &group,
);
let network = rgsaddle::PairNetwork::build(&group, &[[1, 0, 0], [0, 1, 0], [0, 0, 1]], &moves, offsets, 272)?;
let (sites, exits) = network.sites(&spontaneous, &rate)?;
#+end_src

=matched_capture_radius= closes such a chain at its cutoff. Outside the region the capture probability of a field-free crystal is =R/r=, so a hop out to =d= is captured with probability =R/d=, and =R= is fixed by asking the weighted mean of =P r= over the outermost sites to equal it. Capture probabilities are affine in =R=, so two solves give it. On uniform fcc hops with the nearest shells absorbing, =P r= is flat from the second shell out and =R= moves by under 0.3 % between cutoffs of 10 and 16.5 =a/2=. =embed_sphere_return= on the same chain sends a hop that lands just past the sphere back with probability close to 1, and its =P r= rises toward the cutoff.

Which hop classes deserve a free-energy calculation follows from the network itself. Perturb =ln k= of one class at a time in the rate closure, take =d V / d ln k= by central differences, and hand the values to =sensitivity_cover= with the spread of the rate model and the tolerance of the result. It ranks the classes by =abs(d V / d ln k)= and returns how many leading ones to compute so that the rest, all moved by that spread together, shift the result by less than the tolerance. One PAFI path through a class's saddle that ends in the product minimum gives both directions: =FreeEnergyProfile::barrier= from the reactant and =FreeEnergyProfile::reverse_barrier= from the last plane, each with its own error. Their difference is the free-energy difference of the two ends plus whatever the integral drifts along the path, and the errors of the plane averages need not cover that drift. When the difference of the ends is known, for two minima from their zero-temperature energies, =FreeEnergyProfile::balanced= (or =balanced_barriers= on the numbers) takes the forward barrier and the reverse one plus that gap as two estimates of one barrier. It returns their inverse-variance mean, the reverse barrier the gap below it, and the drift, with half the drift added to the error. A path between two equivalent states is balanced against no gap. The two directions then obey detailed balance with the end energies they were balanced on.

The two barriers need not be equally good. The forward one integrates the profile up to the dividing surface and the reverse one past it to the last plane, so each carries the drift of its own stretch. A path integrated again from its other end shows which: the barrier above each end is then read once from the start of an integration and once from its end. When the readings from the start agree and those from the end do not, the drift lies past the dividing surface, and =FreeEnergyProfile::anchored= (or =anchored_barriers=) keeps the forward barrier and its error and puts the reverse barrier the gap below it. Its =drift= is still what the barrier from the last plane adds to the gap, a check on the paths it was not taken from. Rate the isolated reference the same way as the classes it anchors, or the ratio of their rates keeps the drift. One hop class has one dividing plane, so its same =κ= multiplies both directional PAFI rates. In uncertainty draws the two directions share the class draw, with =σ²_ln k = (σ_F/kT)² + (σ_κ/κ)²=; the isolated reference carries its own shared term. Setting =κ= to exactly one and =σ_κ= to zero leaves the former network arithmetic bit for bit.

#+begin_src rust
let cover = rgsaddle::sensitivity_cover(&sensitivity, model_spread, tolerance);
let compute: Vec<usize> = cover.order[..cover.covered].to_vec();
let (profile, _) = rgsaddle::pafi_profile(&surface, &path, &path.arcs(), &pafi)?;
let both = profile.anchored(product_energy - reactant_energy)?;
let (forward, backward, error) = (both.forward, both.reverse, both.error);
#+end_src

The classes left on the rate model should not inherit its bias. Each computed class gives a residual =ln k_computed - ln k_model=, its error and a row of features; the two directions of a class rated from one path share one error, so they enter as one residual, their mean. =calibrate_surrogate= fits =x · β= to the residuals by weighted least squares with weights =1/(σ² + τ²)=. The scatter =τ= is the Paule–Mandel value: zero when the errors already explain the residuals, otherwise the one that brings the weighted sum of squares to its degrees of freedom. A direction still on the model then takes =SurrogateCalibration::shift=, and =SurrogateCalibration::spread= is its error as one more draw from the same population, the spread to hand back to =sensitivity_cover=. =SurrogateCalibration::draw= moves the coefficients within their covariance for error draws. A column of ones fits a common bias. A feature that differs between the two directions of one hop class changes their ratio, so it keeps detailed balance only when that difference is a difference of a function of the state.

#+begin_src rust
let fit = rgsaddle::calibrate_surrogate(&features, &residuals, &errors)?;
let (shift, spread) = (fit.shift(&[1.0]), fit.spread(&[1.0]));
let cover = rgsaddle::sensitivity_cover(&on_model, spread, tolerance);
#+end_src

* Assumptions
The dividing surface is a hyperplane normal to the path, not a variationally optimized surface. The harmonic prefactor uses a caller-chosen block of atoms when the whole-cell Hessian is too large; modes below =10⁻⁴= in the surface's curvature unit are left out of the product. Overdamped sampling assumes the constrained ensemble has been reached after =thermalize= steps. The basin of the path on a plane is the one its in-plane quench from the path reaches, tested at the ends of stretches; a crossing that comes back within a stretch stays in the average. The Bennett–Chandler plateau is whatever record the caller names; a negative =κ= means that record is not a transmission coefficient and no rate is stored. The cell of a frozen search is the cell bound into the surface.

* Copper
The Mishin EAM1 numbers are a validation, not part of the library. The potential is the analytic form already in =tests/mishin_cu.rs= (Mishin, Mehl, Papaconstantopoulos, Voter, and Kress, Phys. Rev. B 63, 224106 (2001), doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.63.224106, Table I). The question is the one posed by Mwungeli, Liu, and Demkowicz, Phys. Rev. Materials 9, 023601 (2025), doi:10.1103/PhysRevMaterials.9.023601: whether the vacancy / self-interstitial recombination volume in Cu falls from about 290 Ω at 300 K to about 160 Ω at 500 K and above because the hop rates toward, away from, and tangential to the vacancy are anharmonic. Crossref records the paper as published 11 February 2025, Phys. Rev. Materials 9, 023601. Their Table 1 gives the zero-pressure lattice parameters 3.631, 3.637, 3.643, 3.649 and 3.655 Å at 300, 400, 500, 600 and 700 K, and cells of 10, 11, 12, 13 and 16 cubic repeats (4000, 5324, 6912, 8788 and 16384 atoms).

Climbing L-BFGS follows the highest image. On the copper band that image was a collision left by the initial path, and the step ran up the wall; the NEB guards drop such a step and do not make it the saddle. The band used here relaxes with FIRE and climbing off, then climbs. A path is kept only when the force is at most 0.01 eV/Å and the zero-temperature integral of the path force reproduces ΔE0.

The path integral is the line integral of ∇E along the polyline through the images. A cubic spline between those images cut the corner off a curved saddle, and the rotation integral came out 14 meV low. On the polyline, ΔE0 and the zero-temperature integral differ by at most 0.0053 eV over 122 rated translation hops on the 8×8×8 cell. The free dumbbell's rotation was not found by the seeds used here, so its ΔF is still not stored.

The free dumbbell translation on the 8×8×8 cell has ΔE0 = 0.090966 eV at the 500 K lattice and 0.094614 eV at the 300 K lattice. The zero-temperature integrals are 0.089934 eV and 0.093511 eV. PAFI gives ΔF(500 K) = 0.094013 ± 0.009186 eV, rate 1.442×10^11 Hz, and ΔF(300 K) = 0.092507 ± 0.005543 eV, rate 3.383×10^10 Hz. The rotation seeded from the ⟨110⟩ guess has ΔE0 = 0.233710 eV at 500 K (integral 0.226944 eV, 6.8 meV low, inside the gate) and ΔE0 = 0.236061 eV at 300 K (integral 0.231614 eV). ΔF(300 K) = 0.201437 ± 0.008689 eV, rate 4.597×10^8 Hz. ΔF(500 K) = 0.164418 ± 0.016586 eV; that error is above 0.01 eV, so the 500 K rotation rate is not within the requested tolerance.
