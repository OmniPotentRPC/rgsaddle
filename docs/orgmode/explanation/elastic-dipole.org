#+title: Elastic dipoles and periodic images
#+options: toc:nil num:nil

* Dipole of a relaxed defect
The sign convention is the one of Varvenne and Clouet, Phys. Rev. B 96, 224103 (2017), doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.96.224103: a dipole \(P\) in an imposed strain has energy \(-P:\epsilon\).  For a fixed periodic cell,

#+begin_example
P = -V (sigma_defect - sigma_bulk).
#+end_example

=ElasticDipole::from_stress= applies that residual-stress formula and symmetrizes the result.  =ElasticDipole::from_energy_strain_gradient= accepts the two homogeneous-strain energy gradients directly, \(P=-(dE_defect/d\epsilon-dE_bulk/d\epsilon)\).  =ElasticDipole::from_forces= computes the symmetric first moment of equilibrated Kanzaki forces.  It refuses a force set with a resultant or torque above its scale-dependent roundoff allowance.  These three routes and the perfect-cell stress subtraction are reviewed by Clouet, Varvenne, and Jourdan, Comput. Mater. Sci. 147, 49 (2018), doi:10.1016/j.commatsci.2018.01.053.

The potential is not part of these types.  A host evaluates the relaxed bulk and defect configurations with rgpot, obtains stress (or homogeneous-strain derivatives) from rgpot strain requests, and passes the results to rgsaddle.  Atomic geometry remains in =linkcell::Cell=; its re-exported minimage displacement is used for the defect separation.  No second neighbour list or minimum-image convention is hidden in the elastic layer.

* Anisotropic Green function
=ElasticConstants= accepts a full \(C_{ijkl}\), conventional Voigt \(C_{IJ}\), cubic \(C_{11},C_{12},C_{44}\), or isotropic Lamé constants.  It checks elastic symmetries and the acoustic tensor.  =AnisotropicGreen= evaluates Barnett's half-circle integral and Barnett's analytic second derivative, D. M. Barnett, Phys. Status Solidi B 49, 741 (1972), doi:10.1002/pssb.2220490238.  There is no finite-difference fallback.

For separation \(r\), source \(P^a\), and target \(P^b\), the interaction is

#+begin_example
E_ab = P^b_ij G_ik,jl(r) P^a_kl.
#+end_example

The periodic trapezoid rule is evaluated on nested angular meshes.  Their difference is the angular error estimate.  The isotropic test compares the result with the closed Eshelby--Kelvin expression.  The cubic test uses the exact =[100]= result of Dederichs and Leibfried, Phys. Rev. 188, 1175 (1969), doi:10.1103/PhysRev.188.1175: \(G_{xx,xx}=1/(2 pi C_{44}r^3)\), while choosing constants that are not isotropic.

* Periodic correction
The \(1/r^3\) image sum is conditionally convergent.  =PeriodicElastic= fixes its order by growing Cartesian spheres, as required when applying the periodic image correction of Varvenne, Bruneval, Marinica, and Clouet, Phys. Rev. B 88, 134102 (2013), doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.88.134102.  It reports the change on removing the outer sphere plus both nested angular errors.  For a pair,

#+begin_example
correction = E_infinite - E_periodic_images
E_corrected = E_atomistic_periodic + correction.
#+end_example

=correct_energy= handles one pair state, =correct_landscape= maps a whole energy landscape, and =correct_rate_catalogue= applies the reactant and saddle corrections before rebuilding the harmonic rate.  Every result carries an absolute error estimate.  The periodic energy includes the cross interaction with every image and one half of each defect's interaction with its own nonzero images.  Consequently a barrier remains correct when a defect dipole changes between the reactant and saddle states.

The smoke cell-size test synthesizes the atomistic long-range contribution from the same periodic sum, then verifies that adding the correction removes its cell drift for several separations and box lengths.  The full copper job is driven by =scripts/benchbot-elastic-dipole.sh=.  It runs every size in =RGSADDLE_REPEATS= and records the field-free and dipole-corrected \(V_{iv}\) estimators as separate rows.

* Copper reference status
The Mishin Cu EAM tests evaluate the vacancy and =[100]= dumbbell residual stresses after relaxation.  No published tensor for those two defects using exactly the 2001 Mishin parameterization and this fixed-cell convention was identified, so the test records the tensors and checks symmetry, route consistency, and cell convergence rather than asserting an unrelated potential's numbers.  Mishin et al., Phys. Rev. B 63, 224106 (2001), doi:10.1103/PhysRevB.63.224106, report defect energetics and elastic constants, not these residual-stress dipole tensors.
