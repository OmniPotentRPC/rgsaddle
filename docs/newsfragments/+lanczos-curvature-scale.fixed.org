A Lanczos Ritz value near zero is now measured against the curvature scale of the estimate, the largest Hessian action norm it has formed, instead of an absolute threshold.
When the Ritz value has no magnitude of its own, the residual stop, the torque window, the relative change of the Ritz value, and the first-product early return divide by that scale.
The Krylov breakdown test is relative to the same scale.
The small tridiagonal eigensolve also measures its convergence and skipped rotations relative to its largest entry.
Multiplying the Hessian by any factor, as a change of energy or length units does, leaves every Lanczos stop and the returned mode unchanged.
